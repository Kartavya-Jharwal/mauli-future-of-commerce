# 04 Explainable personalisation

MAIN PITCH 4/8. Timing: 50 seconds.
The prototype asks what the person wants, their available time and product preferences. It returns one feasible starting step, an explanation and the total price. This translates Fogg's ability component into a simpler task. ELM gives us a reason to pair sensory cues with accessible product facts for shoppers who want to examine the argument. Curation reduces unnecessary comparison without hiding alternatives. Start with inspectable rules and reviewed product content. We can demonstrate the experience before funding an AI service. A later AI layer needs factual and cost testing. The optional chord concept must help understanding, with a readable text alternative.
Sources: LOCAL-06: Future of Commerce — Week 2_ Consumer Behaviour & AI-Personalised Shopping.pptx, slides 11–16 and 34–35; docs/brief/updated-project-brief.md (working recommendation, 10 October 2026), section 4. No prototype completion, uplift or diagnostic validity is claimed. Dosha content remains optional wellness education.

# 05 Practitioner social/live education

MAIN PITCH 5/8. Timing: 50 seconds.
The companion campaign is one short, practitioner-led demonstration around a specific ritual occasion. It shows technique and texture, explains scent honestly and invites the same optional discovery flow. Digital content cannot deliver physical scent or touch, so a spa or sample can complement it where feasible. Positioning emphasises informed ritual practice. Merchandising offers a starter, fuller routine and product-free practice. Pricing shows the total cost and avoids automatic discounting. Customer experience connects explanation to choice and voluntary continuation. A video sending people to Shopify is social-influenced commerce. A live session is the education test, with native platform checkout a later eligibility decision.

# 06 A new metric for ritual continuation

MAIN PITCH 6/8. Timing: 60 seconds.
Our signature metric is Ritual Continuation Rate. At day 21 we ask a consenting recipient whether they practised the agreed ritual in the last seven days. Before launch, we define the practice threshold and eligible response window with the client. The numerator is eligible respondents who meet that threshold. The denominator is eligible responding recipients. We always report response coverage and sample counts because nonrespondents may behave differently. This measures reported practice, not clinical benefit, identity or sales. It can guide the clarity and feasibility of the guidance. Commercial checks remain contribution after acquisition and matured 90-day second purchase. A sale or an email open cannot prove a ritual continued.
Sources: LOCAL-11: Mauli Rituals — Client Challenge Discussion.pptx (classroom synthesis of September 2026 founder Q&A and August 2026 retailer deck), slide 14 (new-metric criteria); docs/metrics/measurement-plan.md (proposed specifications, 10 October 2026), Ritual and cultural measures; project report draft day-21 protocol agreed for consistency. Threshold and window are proposals awaiting validation. Repeat metric includes only buyers with a complete 90-day observation window.

# 07 Pilot economics and decision gates

MAIN PITCH 7/8. Timing: 50 seconds.
A local usability test checks comprehension before any commercial pilot. If the client proceeds, we compare eligible visitors with the existing journey using a holdout where feasible. We pre-agree the primary outcome, duration, cost ceiling and guardrails. Financial scenarios compare no campaign with downside, base and upside adoption. They include net basket, fulfilment, acquisition, support cost, cannibalisation and rollout delay. No forecast is an actual Mauli result. Expansion requires credible incremental contribution covering setup and operating costs within an agreed payback window, alongside acceptable comprehension and staff burden. If assignment cannot support causality, we call the findings observational.

# 08 Recommendation and next decision

MAIN PITCH 8/8. Timing: 40 seconds.
We ask for agreement on one journey and the evidence needed to test it. Immediately, finish the journey audit, reconcile reported figures and build the local discovery demonstration. A small campaign then depends on a named client owner, content review, measurement access and an agreed budget. Longer-term options include reusable knowledge assets and practitioner workflows if demand and account economics support them. Voice and more elaborate motion can remain presentation concepts until they prove value. We exclude a store replacement and a full therapist platform from this coursework MVP. This recommendation supports adaptability through measured increments rather than a feature list.
