# STP and in-depth ICP hypotheses

As of **2026-10-10**. This is an actionable segmentation and interview plan, not a claim that the client has these measured audiences. Evidence register: [sources-channels.csv](sources-channels.csv). Funnel definitions: [channels-and-funnels.md](channels-and-funnels.md).

## Evidence boundaries and targeting decision

**Observed:** existing owned guidance and concern-led products, retail stockists and spa partnerships support research into consumer ritual needs and partner operating needs. Relevant primary pages: [quiz CH02](https://www.maulirituals.com/pages/discover-your-dosha), [body-care CH03](https://www.maulirituals.com/collections/body-care), [stockists CH06](https://www.maulirituals.com/pages/stockist), [spa directory CH08](https://www.maulirituals.com/pages/experience-mauli-spa-rituals). They establish offered touchpoints, not segment sizes or segment profitability.

**Internal/unaudited:** supplied classroom discussion deck [INT04 slide 8](../inputs/Mauli%20Rituals%20%E2%80%94%20Client%20Challenge%20Discussion.pptx.md) attributes core age 35+ to the founder; original retailer deck [INT05 page 9](../inputs/MauliDeckAug26-.pdf.md) names “experimental traditionalists” and attitudes towards quality, culture, purpose and learning. These are founder observation and brand-defined psychographics, not a verified customer distribution. Treat age, household income and ethnicity as research variables, not qualification rules. The former personal dossier has been withdrawn and is not an active source.

**Proposed targeting:** start with existing shoppers who want practical help building a manageable home ritual, and existing spa partners able to test a continuation handoff. Both can be reached through established touchpoints. Explore cultural connection and gifting as bounded additional hypotheses, without a diaspora-first repositioning or new-market business case. Choose targets by observed engagement, repeat contribution and operational feasibility, not by an invented persona's spending power.

**Proposed positioning:** “Mauli helps you choose and continue a considered Ayurvedic-inspired home ritual, with clear guidance on products, steps and their origins.” Partners receive teachable rituals and a supported continuation pathway. This is copy for testing, not an approved medical or product claim. One product or a no-purchase practice can be the correct recommendation.

## Segmentation architecture

Consumer segmentation combines **job/occasion** (everyday routine, specific beauty concern, gifting, post-spa continuity), **knowledge** (new to Ayurveda, familiar, practising), **behaviour** (first-time, repeat, category explorer), **constraints** (money, time, sensory preferences) and **preferred discovery**. Self-described cultural connection can enrich volunteered research; ethnicity alone cannot predict needs or trust.

Business segmentation combines **business model** (spa/hotel, speciality retail, therapist practice, occasion-based corporate gifting), **operating scale** (site/team/throughput rather than assumed revenue), **buying governance**, **treatment/assortment fit**, **procurement/fulfilment needs** and **relationship stage**. Wholesale buyers and end consumers require distinct value propositions and event definitions.

## D2C ICP cards — all proposed hypotheses

### C1: Routine builder seeking feasible self-care

- **Job:** fit one enjoyable, understandable practice into ordinary life; avoid an elaborate routine that becomes another obligation.
- **Psychographics to explore:** values intentional downtime, sensory quality and credible explanation; may enjoy tradition without wanting spiritual terminology. These are possible attitudes, not diagnosed personality traits.
- **Behavioural fit signals:** visits how-to content, returns to ritual instructions, asks how much/how often; existing repeat buyer seeking a next step is a strong reachable test candidate.
- **Trigger/occasion:** disrupted routine, seasonal changes or interest in an evening wind-down; ask the customer rather than infer emotional state from clicks.
- **Entry:** journal, newsletter, existing product collection or optional quiz. **Activation:** chooses one feasible step after useful explanation; later voluntary confirmation of first use.
- **Decision roles:** shopper usually chooses and uses; household member may influence scent or spending. **Constraints:** ask explicit maximum spend and minutes available; prototype can offer 2/5/10-minute choices as design options, not proven segment habits.
- **Barriers:** complexity, sensory uncertainty, shipping cost, dislike of forced bundles, scepticism about wellness claims, discomfort sharing personal information or using voice.
- **Offer:** useful short ritual with transparent total price, product-free alternative and optional audio/text guidance. Avoid performance promises about sleep or anxiety.
- **Retention hypothesis:** practical follow-up supports continued use more than generic discount blasts. Measure voluntary use separately from reorder.
- **Disqualifier:** acute health concern needing professional care; recommendation engine should route beyond product advice. Lack of purchase intent is not a reason to withhold education.
- **Validation:** “What would make this feel manageable?” “What did you actually do after buying?” “Which guidance was unnecessary?” “Would you still use this without an offer?”

### C2: Concern-led premium beauty evaluator

- **Job:** choose a suitable hair/skin/body product with understandable ingredients, use instructions and realistic expectations.
- **Psychographics to explore:** seeks proof, control and quality; open to tradition if explained; may have little interest in dosha categorisation.
- **Fit signals:** comparison/PDP engagement, ingredient or suitability questions, retailer review reading; category repeat purchases. Do not infer income from premium browsing.
- **Trigger:** depletion of an existing product, dissatisfaction with current routine or a gift/sample trial. **Entry:** search, stockist, concern collection, paid creative where current campaigns are confirmed.
- **Activation:** understands why an item fits preferences and selects an intended use step; optional quiz should not obstruct direct purchase.
- **Decision/constraints:** user is buyer or gift recipient; price comparison, fragrance sensitivity, ingredient preferences and limited experimentation budget matter. Capture declared preferences and total delivered price rather than hard-code “affluent.”
- **Barrier:** ingredient uncertainty, unsubstantiated efficacy, mismatch between retail/owned pricing, routine overloading, fear of irritation.
- **Offer/retention:** concise suitability and use guidance; repeat replenishment based on real customer usage, not the dossier's unvalidated depletion estimate.
- **Validation:** “What did you compare?” “What evidence changed your choice?” “Would a dosha result help or distract?” “What would justify the price to you?”

### C3: Spa guest wishing to continue an experience

- **Job:** recreate a specific part of a memorable treatment at home, rather than purchase an entire spa experience.
- **Psychographics:** possible trust in therapist recommendation and high sensitivity to scent/texture; attachment must be discovered individually.
- **Fit signal:** completed partner treatment plus permission to receive continuation guidance. Being a luxury-hotel guest does not establish ongoing spending capacity.
- **Trigger/entry:** therapist explanation, take-home card or partner-approved link after treatment. **Activation:** selects a practical home step with relevant product and instructions.
- **Roles:** guest/user, therapist recommender, hotel gatekeeper; guest gift buyer may differ from recipient. **Constraints:** luggage, travel delivery, language, currency, time and household routine.
- **Barriers:** vague product identification, unavailable local delivery, a handoff that feels like the hotel selling personal data, product differing from treatment memory.
- **Offer:** partner-specific continuation page and optional owned opt-in; therapist credit only with partner agreement. **Retention:** repeat home use, not repeated hotel stays.
- **Validation:** “Which part would you repeat?” “What would the hotel need to explain before you scan?” “Where should the product arrive?”

### C4: Meaningful gift buyer / culturally engaged explorer

- **Job:** give a thoughtful product/practice with a clear story, or connect personally with a tradition in a way that feels respectful and relevant.
- **Psychographics:** motivations may include wellbeing, belonging, curiosity, celebration, aesthetics or family connection; neither South Asian heritage nor residence abroad dictates them.
- **Fit signals:** voluntarily expressed interest in origins or gifting; engagement with occasion content; prior gifts. Segment by the job and self-description rather than a presumed identity.
- **Trigger:** birthday, wedding, festival or personal milestone; do not assume every South Asian customer celebrates Diwali or has inherited Ayurvedic knowledge.
- **Entry:** gift collection, relevant event/content, community recommendation or existing retail. **Activation:** selects a gift/ritual whose meaning and suitability they can explain; recipient activation is a separate first-use event.
- **Roles:** buyer, recipient, possible family/event organiser. **Constraints:** gift budget, delivery date, cultural/language preference, recipient sensitivity and gift presentation.
- **Barriers:** tokenism, homogenised culture, appropriated symbols, opaque origin stories, over-personal questionnaire about somebody else, inappropriate medical suggestions.
- **Offer:** optional context from approved sources, transparent occasion suitability and simple recipient instructions; avoid labelling people “ABCD” unless someone self-identifies and wants the term used.
- **Retention hypothesis:** another gifting occasion or recipient-owned routine; gift-buyer repeat and recipient repeat need different cohorts.
- **Validation:** “What makes the story respectful to you?” “Do you want cultural context at all?” “Who is this for?” “Which details would feel reductive?”

## B2B ICP cards — all proposed hypotheses

### B1: Existing luxury spa / hotel programme operator

- **Firmographics:** premium hotel or destination/urban spa with a staffed treatment operation; prioritise existing verified relationships. Capture geography, sites, treatment rooms, therapist count, service mix and retail capability. No minimum revenue or site count is assumed.
- **Job:** deliver a distinctive consistent treatment that staff can teach and guests value, with reliable supply and acceptable contribution.
- **Buying centre:** spa/wellness director champions; lead therapist evaluates; procurement approves suppliers; finance evaluates margin; hotel general manager/brand team signs off experience; operations owns stock and training. Actual authority varies by property/group.
- **Triggers:** menu refresh, staff training cycle, guest demand, renewal or repeat supply need. **Entry:** existing account relationship or qualified business enquiry.
- **Activation:** trained staff deliver the first agreed pilot ritual; measure staff ability and guest value before expansion.
- **Constraints:** treatment-slot length, room capacity, training availability, stock, lead times, packaging, guest languages, procurement cycle; client must provide MOQ/terms and backbar cost. Do not substitute retail prices for partner economics.
- **Barriers:** training burden, supply variability, treatment overlap, commercial viability, inappropriate claims, guest-data ownership and unauthorised direct marketing.
- **Pilot:** one treatment / one site / limited trained cohort; home continuation only if partner approves. **Retention:** programme stays active and reorder follows utilisation.
- **Qualification:** named operator, genuine need, decision path, capacity to run pilot and agreement on measurement; brand prestige alone is insufficient.
- **Validation:** “What makes a supplier renewal worthwhile?” “Who can approve guest handoff?” “What is cost per delivered ritual?” “How do you confirm therapist proficiency?”

### B2: Premium speciality retailer / beauty buyer

- **Firmographics:** curated beauty retailer, salon retail or online specialist with category fit; record channels/sites, assortment, category depth, replenishment model and geography. Existing stockists are discovery leads, not blanket proof of commercial health.
- **Job:** differentiate assortment and sell through profitably without excessive working capital or staff education burden.
- **Roles:** category buyer owns ranging; merchandiser sets allocation; finance controls terms; store educator and associates explain product; ecommerce team controls PDP/content; legal/quality teams govern substantiation.
- **Triggers/entry:** range review, shopper requests, product launch, existing supplier renewal; stocking enquiry or account introduction.
- **Activation:** staff briefed + first documented sale or agreed demonstration for the relevant store/channel. Sample receipt and PO are commercial setup events.
- **Constraints:** retail margin, tester/sample costs, stock turn, shelf space, launch calendar, content specs, pricing consistency, delivery and returns. No numeric MOQ/margin assumptions.
- **Barriers:** slow sell-through, unclear difference from other brands, inconsistent descriptions, direct-channel discount competition and unmet supply commitments.
- **Pilot:** one focused concern/ritual assortment with short staff guide; track sell-through and gross contribution, separating consumer handoff from partner sales.
- **Validation:** “Which comparison decides ranging?” “What staff question blocks sales?” “Which sell-through threshold and period would you accept?”

### B3: Practitioner / small therapist business

- **Firmographics:** independent practice or small studio offering non-clinical wellness/treatment services; record qualifications, modality, client throughput, retail ability and local geography, without implying all therapists are medical experts.
- **Job:** add dependable products and clear home-care guidance while maintaining professional trust and cash flow.
- **Roles:** owner often buyer/operator; practitioner influences client; distributor may supply. **Trigger:** treatment menu change, client request or repeat demand.
- **Entry:** professional recommendation or business enquiry. **Activation:** completes appropriate product/ritual training and first agreed client demonstration.
- **Constraints:** small inventory budget, limited storage, time away from paid treatments, terms, training format, client suitability and operational scope.
- **Barriers:** minimum order too high, fear of pushy selling, uncertainty on claims and home-use instructions.
- **Pilot:** low-complexity assortment and reusable explanation; subscription/tier programme remains an **internal meeting aspiration**, not confirmed demand [INT01].
- **Validation:** “How much stock risk can you take?” “What would you refuse to recommend?” “Would training or payment terms change adoption?”

### B4: Occasion-based corporate / hospitality gifting buyer

- **Firmographics:** hospitality/event organisation or corporate gifting programme with defined recipients and occasion; capture organisation type, order quantity, geography, fulfilment complexity and branding requirements. Internal luxury/airline references need independent confirmation.
- **Job:** deliver thoughtful consistent gifts on time within an approved budget, with an appropriate story and no recipient friction.
- **Roles:** events/marketing/HR or concierge champions; procurement sources; finance approves; brand team controls packaging; logistics owns delivery. Recipient is an end user, not the account buyer.
- **Triggers/entry:** event, guest welcome, employee milestone, seasonal gifting or account renewal. **Activation:** agreed gifts delivered on time with recipient guidance demonstrably usable.
- **Constraints:** per-recipient landed budget, firm deadline, customisation time, shipping countries, recipient sensitivities, returns and invoicing; validate supplier capacity before promising execution.
- **Barriers:** procurement paperwork, unsuitable content, uncertain lead times, unused gifts, branding conflicts and waste.
- **Pilot/retention:** small approved occasion order; another occasion's reorder is meaningful retention, whereas monthly inactivity can be normal.
- **Validation:** “Who receives it and why?” “Which approval could block it?” “What distinguishes a valued gift from unused stock?”

## Proposed prioritisation matrix

| Segment | Current reachable route | Strategic fit hypothesis | Uncertainty / cost | Initial disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 / C2 existing owned shoppers | Established store and guidance | Useful recommendations can strengthen relevance and repeat use | Baseline, customer usefulness and incremental contribution unknown | Primary coursework and proposed pilot |
| C3 + B1 existing partner programme | Published spa ecosystem | Sensory experience may transfer into owned continuation | Partner consent and attribution required | Secondary bounded pilot |
| B2 current retailer accounts | Published stockist ecosystem | Clear guidance may improve sell-through | Retailer data access and channel conflict | Interviews before building |
| C4 culture/gifting | Existing retail/content possible | Meaningful occasions may be useful | Heterogeneous motivations; no measured audience | Qualitative discovery first |
| B3 therapist tier programme | Internal aspiration | Potential distributed education | MOQ, training economics and demand unproven | Research backlog |
| B4 new major luxury/airline gifting | Internal shortlist / examples | Possible brand experience | Long approval, margins and status unverified | Do not anchor MVP on it |

This ranking is a hypothesis based on reachable existing channels, not a forecast of market size. Score later using consented interview evidence, incremental contribution, partner operational fit, accessibility and maintainability.

## Research protocol and evidence promotion

Proposed first pass: interview a small varied set across existing first-time/repeat shoppers, quiz completers/abandoners and partner operators. A workable coursework starting sample is 8–12 consumers and 3–5 business stakeholders if access permits; this is qualitative learning, not statistically representative validation. Include sceptics and people who prefer direct purchase, not only enthusiasts. Ask about a recent real decision before showing the prototype.

Record voluntary self-description, job/occasion, actual entrypoint, considered alternatives, decision criteria, budget/time limits, first useful moment and barriers. Keep culture and demographic questions optional; no ethnicity inference from names, language or browsing. Obtain permission before recording; aggregate findings and do not publish identifiable participant responses by default. The user's public-repo instruction applies to supplied materials; future participant data requires its own consent.

Promote an ICP claim to **observed interview finding** only with source/session, date, sample size and limitations. Promote to **measured segment behaviour** only with a documented population, defined metric and observation window. Keep attitude, behaviour and financial contribution separate; buying multiple products is not proof of cultural connection or sustained ritual practice.
