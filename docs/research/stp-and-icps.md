Updated 10 October 2026. **Three core umbrellas**, following the owner's confirmed grouping: cultural reconnection; spa-to-home repeated ritual; and premium concern-led consumer plus luxury B2B. The third deliberately contains distinct person and account buying models. These replace the former eight broad cards. They are proposed research profiles, not measured customer segments or approved targeting.

## STP and initial targeting

Segment consumers by declared job, occasion, knowledge, sensory preference, budget, time and decision stage. Optional self-described cultural context can add meaning. Segment accounts by operating model, throughput, buying authority, terms, capacity and service burden. Demographics inform varied recruitment; they do not prove behaviour.

Targeting proposal: demonstrate umbrella 2's manageable home step first, retain umbrella 3's direct concern-led product path as a comparator, and give umbrella 1 its own cultural-content research track. Investigate an existing willing account inside umbrella 3 for a bounded partner continuation pilot. This is a feasibility hypothesis, not an allocation of spend or a new-market repositioning. Advance cultural creative only when participants find it relevant and the client reviews its accuracy.

Positioning hypothesis: Mauli helps people understand, choose and continue a considered ritual that fits their lives. Cultural origins can add meaning; useful instructions and transparent product information still need to answer the decision. For accounts, the corresponding promise is teachable differentiation with demonstrable operating and commercial fit. The proposed thread/chord motif must support comprehension rather than dictate identity or routine complexity.

A person may occupy several jobs: heritage-connected, concern-led and post-treatment. Assign the voluntarily declared primary job to a session, with optional secondary motivation. Do not force a permanent label, sum overlapping populations or treat a gift buyer and recipient as the same person. No invented persona-specific CAC, LTV or uplift is provided.

## Cultural orientation for international teammates

The owner uses ABCD to mean **abroad-born confused desi**. Its conventional expansion is **American-born confused desi**; abroad-born is this project's broader adaptation. Academic discussion documents the conventional usage: [Mudambi, note 3](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17513057.2026.2718877). The wording can judge someone's belonging. Use heritage-connected ritual seeker externally unless an individual chooses another description. Confused is not a psychological finding or necessary trait.

Here, diaspora describes a relationship to an ancestral place while living elsewhere; heritage means what a person identifies as inherited or meaningful. Desi is an informal South Asian identity term; it does not specify everyone's nationality, religion or familiarity with Ayurveda. Ask people what the word means to them. A British-born person with Indian family roots may identify in several ways, with no obligation to use this acronym.

The owner's account of the professor's migration background and of being the only Indian teammate explains why shared vocabulary matters; it does not make those people representative of customers. For the founders, use their public self-description as native Brits of Indian origin rather than assign an ABCD label. Do not assume Indian-origin participants know Sanskrit terminology, want spiritual framing or observe particular festivals; international participants may already be deeply knowledgeable.

Explain Ayurveda/dosha vocabulary in brand-reviewed wellness terms. A dosha experience must not diagnose. Ritual may refer to an intentional repeated practice without compulsory spirituality. Cultural fluency is topic-specific and self-described. The proposed thread motif requires its own reviewed attribution; a person's identity does not automatically authorize symbolic interpretation.

Other diaspora experiences may offer an analogy about adapting or sharing family practices. That is a hypothesis to research, not a claim that Indian practices represent those cultures. Invite personal connections without implying sameness or making purchase a test of belonging.

## Research and evidence promotion

A feasible first qualitative wave is 3–4 participants for each consumer job and 3–4 account stakeholders, including operators and approvers where access permits. This is directional learning, not a representative sample. Recruit existing users, non-buyers and sceptics; include cultural participants who reject reconnection framing and spa guests who never bought products.

1. Reconstruct a recent actual decision before showing the persona: occasion, alternatives, spend, influence, hesitation, purchase and use.
2. Invite corrections and counterexamples; record optional self-description without implying that heritage ought to matter.
3. Compare a relevant prototype with the existing experience. Observe whether the person can explain fit, cost and next step; stated liking alone is insufficient.
4. With consent, follow up on actual use. Reorder, product use and cultural meaning are separate outcomes.
5. Record session/date, recruitment context, evidence type, claim, counterevidence, confidence and validation owner. Keep participant identities outside this public repository; publish only consented/anonymised synthesis.

Promote a hypothesis to an observed qualitative finding only with its source and sample limitations. Claim measured behaviour only with population, event, denominator and observation window. Participants validate their own meanings; the client analytics owner validates rates; account owners validate economics. Every persona has explicit disconfirming tests rather than just supporting interview questions.
