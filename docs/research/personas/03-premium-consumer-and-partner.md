# Umbrella 3 — Premium concern-led consumer and luxury partner account

**One umbrella, two buying models, 10 October 2026.** Maya and Sam are fictional composites, not interviewed customers or real account representatives. Every exact personal/operational detail and numeric constraint is invented to make the research concrete. [Three-umbrella architecture and sources](../stp-and-icps.md).

The shared strategic idea is premium quality that must earn its place through product or commercial fit. The consumer buys for personal use; the account procures for a business. Keep their qualification, journeys, economics, activation and data units separate. Retail, spa, practitioner and corporate gifting remain context variants inside this account model, not additional top-level ICPs.

## A. Maya — concern-led premium consumer

### Demographic scenario and job

Maya is 41, lives in Birmingham and works in project delivery. She has an established beauty routine and is considering a premium hair-care item for the appearance and feel of dry hair. Her trial constraint is **£45 including delivery** and ten minutes once a week for an additional step. These are scenario constraints, not Mauli pricing, typical income or a spend forecast. Her name does not specify ethnicity, religion or Ayurvedic fluency. Gender and occupation are illustrative rather than qualification rules.

Scenario voice, not a quote: “Tell me what it is suitable for, how to use it and why it earns a place beside what I already own.”

Qualification: a specific cosmetic job and willingness to evaluate a premium option. Premium browsing is not proof of wealth. A medical concern requires a different support route; discovery must not diagnose or make unsupported clinical recommendations.

### Psychology and everyday context

Functional need: make an informed selection without duplication, unclear ingredients or excessive effort. Emotional need: confidence and control, rather than persuasion by an unexplained score. Social context: a trusted person's recommendation may matter, but do not invent a status motive. Dream: a reliable, enjoyable routine with fewer disappointing trials. Needs: product purpose, reviewed use instructions, ingredients, credible limits, delivered price and comparison. Wants: enjoyable sensory properties and an optional coherent ritual. Anxieties: paying for vague promises, irritation, an endless upsell and abandoning another product.

Ethnographic prompts concern the actual bathroom shelf, product combinations, previous abandoned trials, where comparison happens and whose advice mattered in a recent purchase. Cultural knowledge and interest remain questions. Heritage can matter without dominating this session's concern-led job.

Emotional triggers could be disappointment with a current product or enjoyment of a sample. Rational triggers include depletion, suitable information, acceptable price and realistic application time. Review popularity, retailer familiarity and price-as-quality inference may affect choice; treat these as testable decision tendencies. Counterbalance them with transparent facts, genuine review context and alternatives, not manufactured scarcity or certainty.

### Journey and influences

Maya enters through category search, product content, a stockist or a personal recommendation. She checks suitability, ingredients, instructions, price and reviews, compares existing products and may ask a retailer or knowledgeable person. Optional ritual guidance may help after fit is clear. Mandatory quiz completion is a hypothesis to challenge, not a requirement.

Alternatives: stay with the current item, alter the technique, choose a lower-cost option or buy at a trusted stockist. A shift from stockist purchase to D2C is not necessarily incremental demand. Preserve channel economics and partner relationships in the assessment.

Preferences to ask: format, fragrance, current routine, time, total spend ceiling, declared ingredient exclusions and desired explanation depth. Do not infer sensitive attributes or health conditions. Declared exclusions do not authorize the system to guarantee allergy safety beyond reviewed product information.

### Offer, content and activation

Propose an understandable product explanation: why it may fit declared preferences, limitations, reviewed instructions, total price and direct access. One item may be enough. Additional steps must have a stated benefit. Avoid guaranteed hair growth, medical before/after promises, diagnostic labels and the assumption that natural ingredients suit everyone.

Activation: Maya selects an intended use step after explaining fit and relevant limits. Selection, comprehension and purchase are distinct events. Quiz completion alone is not activation. Retention is appropriate repeat purchase within a matured cohort, with returns and dissatisfaction counted. Depletion intervals require usage data, not this invented routine.

Trust barriers: inflated claims, opaque terminology, weak comparison, shipping costs, incompatible sensory preferences and pressure to buy a set. Product information comes before spectacle. A chord visual earns a place if it improves explanation; keep a direct readable equivalent. Human support can address what reviewed information does not resolve.

Track declared job, guided/direct route, understood explanation, selected step, purchase, returns, contribution and matured repeat. Information use does not establish that a cosmetic concern improved. No persona-specific CAC or uplift is known.

### Disconfirming interviews

Reconstruct a recent purchase and rejected alternatives. Ask what evidence changed the choice before introducing Ayurveda. Compare the current product page with the proposed explanation. Ask what is missing, whether the quiz helps, what would justify the price and what would cause a return. Test whether preference survives removing premium visuals.

Disconfirm if cheaper alternatives sufficiently solve the job, current pages already work, claims do not answer the expressed need or personalisation adds effort without usefulness. If information clarity is the need, keep the recommendation at that scope. Compliments on a prototype do not demonstrate demand.

The supplied retailer deck p.9 frames quality, realistic expectations and learning; the existing collections and quiz provide actual alternative routes. This supports a concern-led comparator, not a verified customer distribution. Category demand, friction, returns, willingness to pay and profitable acquisition remain unknown.

## B. Sam — luxury partner account champion

### Firmographic scenario and person behind the account

Illustrative account: a premium independent London hotel with **one site, four treatment rooms, eight therapists**, a retail shelf and an established wellness menu. Illustrative champion: Sam, 46, spa and wellbeing director, accountable for guest experience, staffing and department economics. The hotel and all numbers are invented. Sam's demographics do not qualify the account; named real hotels in Mauli's directory are not being impersonated.

Scenario voice, not a testimonial: “Show me the guest value, staff workload and economics of one deliverable pilot.”

The account qualifies through real need, category fit, a decision path, operating capacity and viable economics. Prestige alone is insufficient. Prioritise a verified existing relationship where an account manager can confirm interest; directory presence does not prove a current agreement.

### Firmographic record and ethnographic investigation

Capture legal entity/geography, ownership, sites, service/retail mix, throughput, staff, stock capacity, supplier arrangements, payment terms, return rights, lead times, buying calendar and responsible buyer. Ask for ranges where appropriate and mark unknowns. Do not fill gaps with assumed luxury-hotel averages or publish confidential account records.

Observe or ask how staff learn a treatment, measure product, find instructions, explain unfamiliar vocabulary and escalate stock/guest questions. These operating routines are the ethnographic focus; no field study has yet occurred. Cultural fluency varies across staff. Approved pronunciation and plain-language context may help, but do not make an Indian staff member the default cultural authority.

### Psychographics and organisational incentives

Sam wants a distinctive programme that staff deliver consistently and finance will renew. Functional needs are teachable differentiation, dependable supply and workable contribution. Emotional needs include confidence in recommending an understood product and fewer avoidable escalations. Wants include elegant materials, a compelling story and responsive account support. Dream: guests remember the programme and colleagues sustain it without constant rescue. Anxieties: turnover, stock tied up, slow sell-through, unsupported claims, service failure and losing the guest relationship without agreement.

Triggers include a scheduled menu/range review, renewal, verified guest requests or replenishment. Prestige halos, preference for an incumbent and sunk training costs may shape decisions; these are interview hypotheses. Offer comparable economics and a bounded decision rather than exploit authority or status pressure.

### Buying committee and procurement gates

| Role | Desired outcome | Evidence needed / possible veto |
|---|---|---|
| Spa director/category champion | Differentiation and manageable rollout | Relevant demand, clear use case and staff workload |
| Lead therapist/store educator | Accurate, teachable delivery | Demonstration, reviewed instructions and claims |
| Procurement/account owner | Reliable supply and terms | MOQ, lead times, returns and supplier documentation |
| Finance | Sustainable contribution and cash discipline | Landed costs, labour/testers, stock exposure, payment timing |
| GM/brand/category head | Fit with experience and positioning | Brand presentation, quality and coherent rationale |
| Operations/ecommerce | Correct content and fulfilment | Product data, asset permissions and stock workflow |
| Data/legal owner where relevant | Appropriate permissions | Guest consent, referral/data ownership and approved wording |

Several roles may belong to one person. A champion does not automatically sign a purchase order or approve a D2C handoff. Map actual authority.

Sales cycle: confirmed need → stakeholder review → samples/training → operational/commercial approval → pilot/PO agreement → launch → review and possible reorder. Duration is unknown. Ask for dates, dependencies and decision windows rather than invent a number of weeks. A contractual renewal and a gifting deadline require different schedules.

### Context variants inside this one account model

| Context | Buyer/operator | Economic unit | Constraints | Evidence for continuation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spa/hotel, primary illustration | Director/therapist | Delivered treatment; retail sale recorded separately | Room slots, backbar usage, training, menu overlap | Staff competence, treatment contribution, utilisation, guest comprehension |
| Existing luxury retailer | Category buyer/associate or online merchandiser | Net sell-through by SKU/store/channel | Open-to-buy, shelf space, testers, allocation, returns, calendar | Sell-through, aged stock, explanation quality, net margin |
| Independent therapist | Owner-practitioner | Client visit plus optional product sale | Small inventory budget, storage, training time, professional scope | Usable guidance, manageable initial stock and real reorder |
| Corporate/hospitality gifting | Events/HR/concierge; fulfilment coordinator | Delivered accepted gift per recipient | Deadline, landed budget, customization, recipient suitability | On-time completeness, usability, cost, complaints and occasion reorder |

Corporate gifting has weaker direct support than the published retail/spa ecosystem and remains adjacent. Practitioner programmes are team aspirations, not validated demand. Do not let those variants expand the first pilot. They share an umbrella but cannot share a single margin, cycle or retention assumption.

### Economics for the finance handoff

For Mauli, evaluate net partner revenue less product cost, freight borne by Mauli, commissions, samples/testers, training, account service, discounts and expected returns. Show fixed setup separately. For the account, evaluate net treatment/retail revenue less landed product consumption/cost, variable staff time, consumables, payment fees and any agreed referral costs. Add payment and inventory timing for cash flow.

Do not count backbar consumption as retail stock twice, or wholesale sell-in plus partner sell-through as two Mauli revenues. Compare continuation of the current range/menu, a smaller merchandising improvement and the proposed pilot. Include displaced treatment capacity/shelf space. Gross margin alone does not establish viability. The account must supply its minimum acceptable contribution, stock exposure and review window; no financial thresholds are invented here.

### Entry, activation, offer and retention

Entry: current account conversation, relevant professional introduction or genuine business enquiry. Offer one focused treatment/assortment with training format, reviewed facts, replenishment arrangements, guest instructions, owner and evaluation plan. Partner branding/referral claims require actual agreement.

Account activation: a qualified account agrees a defined sample/pilot and criteria. Operational launch is a separate milestone: staff can deliver/explain the experience and the first appropriate service/sale occurs. An enquiry is neither. Retention is justified renewal/reorder with observed utilisation or sell-through. Inactivity between gifting occasions can be normal.

A guest choosing home guidance belongs to the consumer journey, often umbrella 2, with a separate denominator. Account guest records cannot automatically become Mauli marketing contacts. Measure both sides so D2C gains do not hide partner harm.

### Interview and falsification plan

Interview champion, operator and finance/procurement where possible. Reconstruct the last supplier decision and one attractive proposal that was rejected. Ask who signs, who can veto, what gets delayed, which staff question repeats and what happened to the last sample. Request an anonymised unit example for treatment cost or SKU sell-through. Ask how training, testers, returns and payment terms enter decisions. Role-play a continuation handoff before adding technology.

Disconfirm if nobody owns implementation, contribution fails after service costs, terms do not fit, supply is unreliable, staff cannot support training or D2C referral undermines the account. Guest enthusiasm cannot override operating incapacity. Split financial views by context without multiplying the top-level profiles.

### Evidence and unresolved data

Published spa/stockist pages establish an ecosystem; retailer deck p.8 supports partner positioning. They do not establish account profitability, contract status or capacity. Validate with the client's account owner and partner before placing values in models. Unknowns include margin/terms, volumes, calendar, training cost, utilisation/sell-through, working capital, data permission and acceptable thresholds.

## Umbrella-level synthesis without misleading aggregation

Present U3 together as premium fit, then branch immediately: **personal product fit** versus **commercial account fit**. Consumer age, preferences and use constraints never substitute for account qualification. The account champion's emotions help understand a decision, but the firmographic and committee evidence decides feasibility. Aggregate neither population nor revenue twice. The common creative principle is specific, credible explanation; the conversion event and commercial test depend on the buying unit.
