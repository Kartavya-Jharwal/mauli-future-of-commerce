# Umbrella 2 — Alex, treatment-to-home routine continuer

**Fictional research composite, 10 October 2026.** Personal facts, spending limits and routines are invented design scenarios, not Mauli customer statistics. [Architecture and evidence](../stp-and-icps.md).

## Concrete scenario

Alex is 44, lives in Manchester, works full time and occasionally visits London. A partner gave Alex a spa treatment for a birthday. That gift does not establish a luxury lifestyle. Alex liked one particular sensory step and wants to repeat it at home. Available time is **five minutes on three evenings a week**, with **£55 for an initial product including delivery** in this scenario. Gender, ethnicity and family structure are intentionally unspecified: the job does not require them. Recruit across those differences instead of claiming false precision.

Scenario voice, not a testimonial: “Which part can I repeat at home, and what do I actually need for it?”

Routine building is folded here. A visitor arriving through practical content without a spa visit has the same feasible repeated-practice job, but lacks treatment memory and therapist trust. Record post-treatment versus self-directed entry; do not assume equal conversion. A spa visitor who wants no home practice does not qualify merely by attending.

## Ethnographic context and motivations

Ask where products live, who shares the space, whether fragrance affects another person, when routines get interrupted, whether instructions are read on a phone with wet hands and what happened to previous unused products. These are observation prompts, not research findings. Cultural knowledge may be extensive, minimal or irrelevant; explain terms and allow practical-only guidance.

Functional need: a repeatable step with clear product identification, quantity, timing and instructions. Emotional need: preserve a cared-for moment without making self-care another task. Social need may be minimal: the ritual can remain private. Dream: a small enjoyable routine that survives ordinary weeks. Wants: the remembered scent/texture, beautiful instructions and freedom from effort. Needs: feasibility, product-specific guidance, transparent cost and suitability information. Anxieties: wrong product, forgotten instructions, unnecessary purchases or attempting a professional technique without appropriate guidance.

Emotional trigger: recognising the experience. Rational trigger: knowing the exact suitable home-use item and next step. Novelty enthusiasm and trust in a therapist may influence initial choice; neither proves continued use. Distinguish the professional treatment from the step appropriate at home. A product cannot be promised to reproduce every aspect of the spa experience.

## Actual choice process to investigate

1. Alex asks what was used. A therapist identifies one home step without pressure.
2. An agreed card/link opens readable guidance; contact details are optional.
3. Alex checks product, instructions, delivered price and whether delivery home is preferable to luggage.
4. Alternatives include using something already owned, buying this item, booking another treatment or doing nothing.
5. Optional follow-up supports use and asks what fitted life instead of pushing a complete collection.

Influences: therapist explanation, Alex's sensory memory, a household member's fragrance preferences and trusted information. Partner permission and customer-data ownership matter: a guest record is not consent to Mauli marketing. Prototype with fictional partner details until a real handoff is agreed.

Preferences to investigate include text/video/audio, desired guidance depth, scent, texture, language, time, current products, budget and follow-up frequency. Do not infer stress, health conditions or ongoing spending power from a spa visit.

## Entry, offer and activation

Primary proposed entry: approved therapist handoff. Secondary: owned how-to content, newsletter or existing instructions. A self-directed routine builder can qualify through declared purpose, not necessarily through the same campaign.

Offer hypothesis: one appropriate product with a simple practice and transparent total cost. Two/five/ten-minute formats are design options to test, subject to reviewed product instructions, not established audience habits. Provide a product-free alternative. Repeated use should reflect product guidance and customer preference rather than a compulsory daily streak.

Activation: chooses a feasible step after instruction and can explain how to perform it. A selection/save event and comprehension are separate measures. Optional first-use confirmation is a later event. Quiz completion, account signup and bundle purchase do not substitute for activation.

Retention: reported practice and matured replenishment separately. Existing-product use can be educational success with no immediate revenue. A repeat purchase might be a gift rather than ongoing practice. Partner retail sales and Mauli D2C orders require separate attribution and must not be double counted.

## Creative and trust requirements

Show the exact step with readable visuals/text; prioritise identification and home context. Optional motion can connect the treatment moment with the home step if it helps. Maintain a reduced-motion/direct instruction route. Voice is useful only if it improves guidance without adding friction.

Trust barriers: treatment product unavailable online, missing quantities, differing scent expectations, unclear shipping, inappropriate health implications, upselling and uncertainty about the hotel's relationship to Mauli. A warm explanation must still say what is and is not part of the recommendation.

Measure approved source ID, instructions viewed, selected step, declared time fit, optional first use, returns reason and matured repeat. Compare post-treatment and self-directed cohorts. Ask staff about explanation time and operational burden. Avoid treating scan count as evidence of a routine formed.

## Disconfirming research

Ask which part they would repeat, where it fits tomorrow, what they would remove, and whether the product matters without the treatment memory. Reconstruct the last spa product brought home. Test recall and actual use a week later. Observe a realistic instruction task, not only a visual preference rating.

Reject continuation as the central lever if guests want only therapist/place-based value, cannot fit the practice, own adequate substitutes or find the handoff intrusive. Improve instructions if enthusiasm coexists with poor understanding. Polite appreciation is not demand.

## Mauli-specific evidence and unknowns

The Mauli spa page describes treatment formats and offers a home-spa route, supporting a concrete continuation question. Retailer deck p.9 supports investigating demanding schedules and quality. Neither establishes a conversion leak. Client/partner validation is required for guest volume, handoff consent, availability, home/professional product differences, continuation, incremental contribution and training burden.
