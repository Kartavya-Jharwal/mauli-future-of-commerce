1. Executive Snapshot
Mauli Rituals is an independent British luxury wellness brand built on Ayurvedic principles, founded 
in London in 2015 by Anita and Bittu Kaushal. Born from family heritage — Ayurvedic doctors, 
herbalists and plant-medicine farmers — and from the loss of their daughter in 2012, whose dream 
was "to help others feel connected and confident in living." Every product is made in England from 
India's refined botanicals, formulated natural and science-backed, and organised around rituals 
rather than single categories.
The brand's positioning in one line, in the founders' words: "authentic luxury" — small-batch, 
natural, heritage-rooted products that major luxury houses and hospitality brands (Bulgari, 
Lamborghini, Range Rover, Emirates, Four Seasons, Nobu, One&Only) choose to associate with 
because of that authenticity. The deck states the ambition plainly: "The next chapter of Mauli is 
about a world-leading wellness brand rooted in Ayurvedic wisdom."
The core challenge for students: Mauli is a deeply human, story-rich, multi-sensory brand that has 
barely touched AI, AR/VR or emerging commerce tech — and wants to integrate them without 
flattening the ritual into a SKU, and without losing the personal touch that is its entire value 
proposition.

3. Why Now — Market Momentum (from the Aug 2026 retailer deck)
The deck frames Mauli as sitting "at the intersection of Ayurveda, Beauty, Longevity and Modern 
Wellness" — and deliberately not reliant on a single SKU or platform for growth:
• Global wellness economy: $6.8 trillion in 2024, forecast to reach ~$9.8–10 trillion by 2029 
(Global Wellness Summit '25) — now larger than tourism, sports and the green economy.
• Global Ayurveda market: ~$24 billion in 2026 projected to over $85 billion by 2033 — roughly 
19.7% CAGR, one of the fastest-growing sectors within preventative wellness.
• Demographic tailwind: by 2050, one in five people globally will be over 60; spending shifts to 
longevity, vitality and quality of life.
• Category signal: consumers are "no longer simply buying beauty products — they are investing 
in longevity, resilience and preventative wellbeing"; demand for sleep and nervous-system-
health solutions is growing globally.

4. Who the Customer Is (deck psychographics)
The deck names the target customer "experimental traditionalists" — a precise creative brief for any 
personalization or AI-concierge work:
• Enjoy exploring cultures and art; seekers of authentic luxury.
• Want eco-luxe products with purpose.
• Want results, and can afford to search for quality.
• Seek products that help them thrive in demanding schedules.
• Want to learn and grow — Mauli's Ayurvedic knowledge appeals to them.
• Do not expect "youth in a bottle" — they want efficacy, safety, and formulas that work with 
their skin and age.
On the call, Anita confirms the core customer is 35+; when younger audiences engage, "they have 
absolutely loved the product." Teresa Chung, a leading Chinese influencer and RED/Xiaohongshu 
advocate for the brand, is quoted in the deck: "Love everything Mauli does."

6. Brand Story & Non-Negotiables
These are the "non-negotiables" the course challenge must preserve while adding tech:
1. Authentic luxury — best natural ingredients, small-batch, made in England. Chosen by 
Lamborghini and Range Rover because "you can feel there was something more."
2. Three pillars — Look good. Feel good. Do good. Products must deliver all three; the founder has 
discontinued ~5 products that weren't best-in-class ("if I don't need them, I'm not going to sell 
them").
3. The Mauli thread — every boxed product includes a blessed red thread made by an NGO in India 
(symbol of protection, connection, and well-being). Giving is built into the product.
4. No dilution, ever — spas are refused diluted / cheaper formulas, even though it hurts economics. 
Only exception ever considered: airline amenity kits (Emirates), because sub-£1 unit pricing is 
impossible otherwise — and even those were reformulated, not weakened.
5. Give more in value than you take — the dosha / mind-body questionnaire on the site returns 
genuinely useful self-care content with no sales push. "That's not marketing hype, we really do 
care."
6. Founder voice — storytelling is the brand moat. But Anita is deliberately stepping back from being 
the face ("it makes the brand too vulnerable") — the brand needs a way to carry its story without 
the founder at the forefront.

7. Business Model Reality
The D2C-vs-partnership dilemma (spelled out in the post-call debrief)
• Brand partnerships (Lamborghini, Range Rover, Emirates) are credibility halo, not profit — 
"nice to have, but not that profitable." No compounding profit effect.
• D2C is 61% of mix and the profit engine, but barely breaks even on first order given discounting 
and rising Meta costs.
• Retail economics are brutal: Space NK wants 65% margin + 10–15% marketing contribution + 
free product + paid placement — viable only for large brands.
• Spa / amenity channel is easier margin-wise and "on brand," but scales slowly.
Discount dependency
• Two annual sale events (September birthday sale, Black Friday, both −20%) have skewed 
revenue and conditioned customers; the brand wants to wean customers off discounting — but 
slowly.
Channel incoherence risk
The brand does D2C + spa + retail + amenities + partnerships simultaneously, each speaking to 
different audiences — "for all of those to look coherent can be quite challenging." Anita's self-
identified worst-case marketing scenario: "not always being as coherent as we would like… and not 
feeling authentic. Also not necessarily reaching the right people."
Audience gap
• Core 35+ customer; TikTok (and younger cohorts) deliberately untouched — "if we do it, we 
need to do it properly" (discounting culture, posting cadence, spreading too thin). Statistic 
Anita cited: older TikTok users shop Saturday nights.
• Social growth (36k → 105k in a year, outsourced to a brilliant manager in Thailand who uses AI 
content) is real — but Anita's worry: "is she building the audience, or just the numbers? … what 
we now need is community."
8. What the Client Explicitly Asked the Class to Solve
Direct brief items from the call (Anita framed these as open questions and "a task"):
1. AI without losing the personal touch. "We've seen how you translate that ritual and our 
messaging with AI and how we don't lose the personal touch." How does an AI shopping agent 
represent a multi-sensory ritual without flattening it to a single SKU? (Her own digital agency 
admitted: "they don't know.")
2. Personalization at scale. The brand has deep mind-body type (dosha) knowledge — three types 
(Vata / Pitta / Kapha archetypes), an on-site questionnaire, and rich follow-up content. "So much 
behind it that would help you create something personalized… that may help with AI. Where we 
would see it, that's for you to tell us."

3. Limited resources — where to execute? "If we have limited resource, then where's the best place 
for us to execute and how is something I'd love to learn from you."
4. Emerging tech for communication & retail. Course scope named by the instructor: AR/VR,

the non-negotiables intact.
5. New metrics. The instructor's framing, endorsed by Anita: the metrics used today "in the next 10 
years might just not work" — an opportunity to derive new metrics the market hasn't seen yet.
6. A concrete B2B automation opportunity (volunteered by the client): build a community of at-
home therapists (mobile massage / skin / hair practitioners who constantly ask to buy products and 
do treatments). Needs: tiered automated purchasing, subscription potential, and vetting / brand 
protection — "we will do it, it's just a question of when… if you thought that was interesting, I could 
send you more details."
7. Community over follower-counting. Converting the 105k Instagram audience into a genuine 
community.
Framing the students' options (per the instructor): choose a B2B path, a B2C (direct) path, or 
integrate all paths into one workflow. Mindset flip required: not "how's the brand doing on social?" 
but "how do you automate and amplify the non-negotiables that make this brand flourish in its own 
habitat, using tech?"

12. The Challenge in One Paragraph
Mauli Rituals is a 10-year-old independent British Ayurvedic luxury brand that luxury giants  
seek out, not the other way round. It is profitable-ish, award-laden, and deeply loved by a 35+  
audience — and it has almost no technology in its stack beyond boosted posts and AI-assisted  
Instagram content. Its founder's brief: show us how AI, AR/VR, and emerging commerce can  
carry a multi-sensory, story-rich ritual to new audiences — without flattening it into a SKU, 
without discount-led growth, without losing coherence across our five channels, and ideally  
with new metrics that measure what actually makes this brand work. Do it with limited  
resources, and tell us where to start.
