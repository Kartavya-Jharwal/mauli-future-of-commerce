## Page 3

The behavioural case
Why sensory marketing wins the consumer
Senses are the shortest route from stimulus to emotion to memory and purchase decisions are made there first.
~90%
of purchase decisions are driven by 
subconscious, emotional processing 
rather than deliberate reasoning 
(Zaltman, 2003; Lindstrom, 2008).
65% vs 10%
People recall visual content three 
days later at 65%, versus ~10% for 
text alone (HubSpot visual content 
research).
+30%
Revenue uplift reported by brands 
engaging three or more senses in 
experience design, versus one-sense 
branding (Lindstrom, Brand Sense, 
2005).
"We feel before we think. Brands that design for the senses design for memory and memory is where preference lives."
paraphrased from Krishna (2012), Journal of Consumer Psychology

Sense 1 of 5 · Vision
The shelf that watches back
In market now
• Nutella Unica (Italy): an algorithm generated 7 million 
unique jar designs. Sold out in ~30 days each jar became a 
shareable visual object.
• Amazon Fresh & Walmart: dynamic shelf-edge screens 
swap pricing and promo by time of day, footfall and basket 
data.
• Warby Parker: AR try-on in its app converts browsing to 
purchase by removing the 'fit uncertainty' barrier in 
eyewear.
On the roadmap
• Emotion-adaptive displays: screens that read dwell time 
and facial response to re-rank what they show.
• AI 'store of one': planograms rebuilt per shopper 
segment, per hour.
• AR as default: beauty, furniture and auto already piloting 
camera-first commerce.
Ask the room: when does personalised visual merchandising become surveillance?

Sense 2 of 5 · Hearing
The three-second brand real-world cases
In market now
• Netflix 'ta-dum': one of the most recognisable sonic logos in the 
world, designed to trigger anticipation in under 3 seconds.
• Mastercard: dropped its name from the logo and built a full sonic 
brand architecture melody for ads, point -of-sale sounds, ringtones.
• Starbucks: licenses and curates playlists to match daypart and 
mood, turning the store into a branded audio environment.
• McDonald's 'I'm lovin' it': a five-note mnemonic that travels 
across 100+ markets with near -universal recall.
On the roadmap
• Adaptive soundscapes: store audio that shifts tempo and 
genre in response to real-time crowd emotion and queue 
length.
• Voice-first commerce: assistants that hold your brand 
voice, accent and emotional register.
• Spatial audio AR: audio anchors in physical space  hear 
the promotion when you walk past the shelf.

Sense 3 of 5 · Smell
The most memory-linked, least digitised sense real cases
In market now
• Singapore Airlines: 'Stefan Floridian Waters' a signature 
scent worn by crew and infused into cabins, consistently 
ranked in brand-experience lists.
• Abercrombie & Fitch: its Fierce fragrance was pumped 
through stores to anchor the brand to a specific teen 
demographic and mood.
• Disney: pumped vanilla scent on Main Street USA and 
baking smells near confectionery windows to extend dwell 
time.
• Lush: unpackaged products make the shop smell the 
advertising scent is the primary media plan.
On the roadmap
• Digital scent: AI models of molecular structure (e.g. 
Osmo) promise scent that can be generated, transmitted 
and printed.
• Scent wearables for VR: headsets with scent cartridges 
for immersive retail and training.
• Programmable scent zones: HVAC-integrated diffusion 
that changes by aisle, hour, or even shopper profile.

## Page 14

Sense 3 of 5 · Smell
Smell 2030 from ambience to data layer
Scent-as-code
If digital scent transmission becomes viable, brands could 
email a fragrance sample or deliver a 'new car smell' with a 
vehicle configurator.
Olfactory personalisation
Scent profiles linked to loyalty data the coffee shop that 
smells different for the morning commuter versus the 
afternoon remote worker .
Memory hook: Herz (2004) showed smell is the sense most directly wired to emotional memory yet hardest to deliver digitally. 
That tension is the opportunity.

Sense 4 of 5 · Touch
Closing the haptic gap real-world cases
In market now
• Apple unboxing: friction curves, matte-black paper, 
magnetic lids each touchpoint is measured to signal 
precision and care.
• Dyson: material honesty (robust plastics, soft-touch 
finishes) tells the engineering story before the motor is 
turned on.
• Samsung: haptic feedback language in Galaxy devices 
carries brand identity through vibration patterns.
• Tanvas: texture screens let shoppers 'feel' fabric on a 
tablet, used in apparel retail pilots.
On the roadmap
• Haptic gloves: SenseGlove and Meta research let 
shoppers feel products in VR fitting rooms.
• Programmable materials: packaging and surfaces that 
change texture on cue.
• Touch telepresence: remote 'feel before you buy' for e-
commerce's biggest sensory deficit.

## Page 17

Sense 4 of 5 · Touch
Touch 2030 from surface to signal
Packaging as interface
Surfaces that warm, cool or texture-shift based on delivery 
timing or user context the box becomes part of the product 
experience.
Haptic search
Online browsing with texture feedback for fabric, finish and 
weight reducing the return-rate penalty of touch-free 
shopping.
Memory hook: Peck & Childers (2003) found touch before purchase increases perceived ownership and valuation — the 
'endowment effect' begins at the fingertip.

Sense 5 of 5 · Taste
The AI flavourlab real-world cases
In market now
• Coca-Cola Y3000: generative AI predicted flavours
consumers would love in a 'future' context launched as a 
limited drop with social conversation baked in.
• Heinz A.I. Ketchup: used AI image-to-concept to explore 
what ketchup could look like, then taste-tested the most 
promising outputs.
• Nespresso: flavour profiling and machine learning guide 
pod recommendations based on past purchases and time -
of-day patterns.
• Häagen-Dazs: in-store sampling tied to weather data ice 
cream flavours pushed on hot days through geo-targeted 
promos.
On the roadmap
• Computational flavour design: models mapping 
molecular combinations to emotional response, not just 
taste.
• Taste-digital crossover: electric taste interfaces for 
remote tasting demos.
• Food as media: limited AI-designed drops engineered for 
social conversation as much as palate.

Emotion-first flavour

The sixth frontier · Mind & context
The sense AI made real-world cases
In market now
• Spotify AI DJ: combines recommendation data with a 
synthetic voice to create a personalised radio host —
context (time, mood, history) becomes content.
• TikTok algorithm: watches micro-behaviour (dwell, 
rewatch, share) to infer emotional state and serve the next 
dopamine-calibrated video.
• Nike Run Club: adapts coaching audio to pace, weather 
and calendar context the product becomes a situational 
companion.
• Amazon: 'customers also bought' and anticipatory 
shipping turn purchase history into a predictive context 
layer.
On the roadmap
• Context-aware retail: stores that sense situation and 
knowledge state (with consent) and adapt every sense.
• Affective computing: real-time emotion detection tuning 
music, lighting and scent in a shop.
• Neuromarketing scale: EEG and eye-tracking move from 
lab to live environment.

The sixth frontier · Mind & context
Context 2030 from segment to individual
The segment of one, physical
Retail environments that reconfigure sensory mix (light, 
sound, scent) based on your loyalty profile, time since last 
visit, and real-time biometrics (opt-in).
Knowledge-state marketing
Systems that detect whether you are browsing, comparing, 
or ready to buy and shift message depth and modality 
accordingly.
"The most sophisticated sense is not smell or taste it is the sense of what someone needs right now. AI is building that."
paraphrased from industry commentary on contextual intelligence

Case study
Apple a sensory ecosystem, not just a product
Apple does not rely on one sense. It orchestrates all five plus context into a single, coherent memory structure.

Clean white stores, uniform lighting, 
product-as-sculpture displays. Every pixel 
of packaging is designed.
Touch
Matte-black paper, magnetic lid snap, 
aluminium coolness — unboxing is a 
choreographed tactile event.

Smell
The 'new Mac' smell a mix of materials 
and manufacturing has become a nostalgic 
trigger for owners.
Context
iCloud, Handoff, and AI recommendations

Result
One of the highest brand -equity scores 
globally, built on sensory consistency as 
much as engineering.

Wrap-up
Key takeaways & references
Takeaways
• Senses buy memory; AI buys timing and personalisation. The winners combine both.
• Sound and scent are the most under -priced brand codes relative to their memory 
impact.
• Touch is e-commerce's biggest deficit — haptics is the investment to watch.
• Context (knowledge + situation) is now a designed sensory input, not an accident.
• Sensory power raises manipulation stakes — ethics is a design requirement, not a 
footnote.
